2 Stage 1: RNN — one word at a time

An RNN reads one word at a time. After each word, it rewrites one small note. At the end, the whole sentence lives in that one note.

Two problems

It forgets. The note is rewritten so many times that early words fade. "Riya" and later "she" lose their link. It is slow to train. Word 2 must wait for word 1, so the GPU works one step at a time.

LSTM and GRU reduced the forgetting. They did not remove it. The one-by-one reading stayed.

3 Stage 2: Attention — look at every word

Attention keeps the whole page open. To understand "she", the model looks directly at every earlier word. Each word gets a weight, and the weighted mix builds the meaning.

Query is the question. Key is the label on each word. Value is the content. Multi-Head Attention runs several scans side by side. One head tracks names, one tracks grammar, one tracks position.

Attention solved forgetting. Early attention still sat on top of RNNs, so slow reading remained.

D

4 Stage 3: Transformer — all words at once

In 2017, the paper "Attention Is All You Need" removed the RNN completely.

A Transformer block
  • Multi-Head Attention
    • the looking step, words gather information
  • Feed-Forward Network
    • the thinking step, each word processes alone
Stack many blocks
  • early blocks learn grammar
  • deep blocks learn meaning and reasoning

The model reads all words in parallel. Positional encoding tells it which word came first. Today, models like LLaMA use RoPE for this job.

Chat models are decoder-only. Reading is parallel. Writing stays one word at a time. That loop is autoregressive generation. It matters later for the KV Cache.

Why it won: all words process in one step, so GPU cores stay busy. Training that took weeks on RNNs took days on Transformers.

5 Stage 4: Scaling — bigger is better, up to a point

Scaling grows three things. Parameters are the internal knobs. Data is the training text. Compute is the GPU time.

  1. 2020Scaling Lawsquality grows smoothly with compute, no wall
  2. 2020GPT-2about 1.5B parameters
  3. 2021GPT-3about 175B parameters, 100× bigger in one year
  4. 2022Chinchillabig models were undertrained, grow data with parameters

Emergent abilities appear only at large size. Then Chinchilla corrected the course. A smaller model trained on much more data can beat a bigger model trained on less.

The cost problem

A dense model activates every parameter for every word. A 100-word answer runs all 175B parameters 100 times. Doubling the size doubles the cost of every answer.

6 Stage 5: MoE — big knowledge, small running cost

MoE = many experts + a router that picks a few.

Only the thinking step changes. One Feed-Forward Network becomes many expert networks. Attention stays the same.

Mixtral 8x7B holds 47B of knowledge. It runs near the cost of 13B. DeepSeek-V3 goes further: 671B total, about 37B active.

The problems with MoE

Memory: all experts stay loaded. MoE saves compute, not memory. Load balancing: the router must spread the work, or some experts never learn. Training complexity: routing makes training less stable.

7 Stage 6: New Directions — what happens now

Attention compares every word with every other word. Doubling the input makes it four times costlier. The KV Cache also eats memory.

Input length 1,000 2,000 4,000 8,000
Attention cost 1 4 16 64
Mamba cost 1 2 4 8

Mamba keeps a running memory like an RNN. It decides what to keep and what to throw away. Far-away information survives better than in the old RNN.

The same Transformer now reads images, audio, and video as tokens. And models can think longer before answering. That is scaling at inference time.
